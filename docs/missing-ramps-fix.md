# The ramps SkyMap never had

Planned and implemented 2026-08-31, from a reader report.

**Outcome vs. plan.** The plan assumed the ramp was in our Overpass response under a name
we weren't matching, or at worst unnamed in OSM and recoverable by attaching a curated name
to a traced polygon. Neither was true. The reported ramp is **not in OpenStreetMap at all**
— no building, no `amenity=parking`, nothing — and it has no Hennepin County parcel under
its street address either. The plan's step "borrow the OSM footprint and name it" had
nothing to borrow, and the shape of the fix changed: an overlay entry carries a verified
*position* and no outline, because the outline is the one thing nobody publishes.

Two things the plan didn't foresee:

- **Two of the four remaining municipal ramps are underground, inside buildings we already
  have.** LaSalle at 10th occupies the lower levels of the downtown Target store. A ramp is
  not always a building of its own, and the skyway link that matters is the host's.
- **The invariant caught the real bug.** The first curated ramp failed
  `tests/router.test.mjs`'s "every building must be reachable from at least one other" —
  correctly. A ramp you can find and cannot walk out of is a worse answer than a missing
  one, so `connectsTo` became a required, separately-sourced field rather than an optional
  extra.

## Context

A reader wrote: *"I can never seem to get the Hennepin @ 10th ramp to show up."*

They were right, and it wasn't a search-matching problem. The ramp is real: **Hennepin at
10th**, 935 Hennepin Avenue, municipal, 880 spaces, open 24/7, skyway-connected. SkyMap had
no record of it.

## The systemic bug

`scripts/fetch-osm.mjs` asked Overpass for `way["building"]["name"]`.

Parking is also absent from the `AMENITY` list, so a ramp could only ever reach the dataset
by being a *named building* in OSM. Ramps are the worst-served category downtown: mappers
trace the parcel and move on. **27 of the 50 parking structures inside the bbox carry no
name**, and not one of them had ever been extracted. The category whose name a driver
actually knows is the category OSM is least likely to record.

Below that sat a second defect. `onPosition` in `src/main.ts` carried a comment explaining
that *"am I parked in this ramp?"* and *"where would I join the skyway?"* are two different
questions with two different distance budgets — and then answered both from
`mainNetworkBuildings()`. That list exists to stop the app offering a trip that ends in "No
route found". It is the right list for choosing where to start walking and the wrong one for
noticing where a car is: **four of downtown's ramps sit off the main component**, so Save My
Ramp was silently unavailable at every one of them.

## What shipped

- **`data/parking-overlay.json`** — curated ramps, two independent sources each, operator
  winning on conflict, same discipline as `poi-overlay.json`. Three entries: Hennepin at
  10th, LaSalle at 10th, 11th Street Underground.
- **`scripts/apply-parking-overlay.mjs`** — merges them after extraction, refuses an entry
  that can't be re-checked or can't be walked out of, and reports an entry as retirable when
  OSM catches up rather than emitting a second ramp on the same corner. Wired into
  `npm run data:refresh`.
- **`data/parking-candidates.json`** — the worklist, generated by the extractor, which now
  asks for parking structures whether or not they are named. Candidates deliberately do not
  become buildings on their own: an unnamed ramp is a grey shape nobody can search for.
- **`parkedAt`** in `src/ramp.ts` — the "am I parked here" question, asked of every parking
  building, at the tight 60m budget the prompt was always meant to use.
- **Point geometry for untraced buildings** in `src/map.ts`. A `Polygon` with an empty ring
  is invalid GeoJSON and costs the whole source; such a building is drawn as a labelled
  point instead. `distanceToFootprint` already fell back to the centroid.

## Why the outlines are empty

Drawing a plausible rectangle around a geocoded address would invent the single fact we
don't have, and it would look exactly like a surveyed one. An entry with no footprint is a
*located* building rather than an *outlined* one — it routes, it labels, and it answers "am
I parked here" a little less precisely, which is the truth about what we know. `footprint`
is filled in only from a cited traced source; the candidates file exists to supply them.

## Left undone

- **Plaza ramp** (117 S 12th St) — name and skyway link sourced, position not. The address
  geocodes into the North Loop, two kilometres wrong. A ramp in the wrong place is worse
  than a missing one.
- **11th & Marquette** (1111 Marquette Ave) — position sourced, no skyway link found. This
  app has nothing useful to say about a ramp you can't walk out of.
- **The other ~24 unnamed structures**, mostly private and commercial, sitting in
  `data/parking-candidates.json` waiting for a name from two sources.
